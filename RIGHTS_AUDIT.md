# Rights audit — the whole corpus, text by text

Every text directory in `texts/` was opened and judged: **290 entries**. Each
line names the work, what its rights situation actually is, and what decision
that leaves us. The evidence behind any line is in the per-section files under
`ocr/0-recon/rights-audit/`, which are longer and quote what was found.

Audited 2026-08-14 by ten agents working from a shared charter. **The governing
rule was that the file settles it** — every verdict here comes from opening the
actual source and reading its title and copyright page, not from `metadata.json`
and not from what the work is known to be. That mattered: several entries
contradict our own records, and a few contradict decisions already taken.

US law governs, because Enchiridion publishes from the United States. A work
published before 1931 is clear. **For a translation, the translation is what we
publish, so the translation's date governs** — not the age of the original.

## What the five words mean

**copyrighted** — the work itself is in copyright. No translation solves this.
Only permission, expiry, or leaving it out.

**translation needed** — the work is free and only this English rendering is
owned. The choice was never whether to have the text; it is whether to have
*this* translation. Our own translation fixes it permanently.

**undetermined** — genuinely unsettled, with the thing that would settle it
named. Not a soft no; a real question.

**no source** — nothing in the directory to judge, or the file is not the work
it claims to be.

**public domain** — clear. Nothing to do.

## The count

- 41  copyrighted
- 16  translation needed
- 26  undetermined
- 3  no source
- 204  public domain

**204 of 290 are clear today.** Of what is not, 16 can be fixed by translating, and
41 cannot be fixed by any work we can do alone.
