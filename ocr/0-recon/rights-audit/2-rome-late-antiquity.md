I audited all 33 text directories under `texts/2-rome-late-antiquity/`. I opened
a source PDF, EPUB, or HTML file for 27 of them; 6 (the five Aristotle logic
treatises other than *Categories*, plus *On the Sphere and Cylinder*) have no
source file at all, only markdown, so I judged those from the translator/date
named in the markdown's own front matter and confirmed the translation against
the historical record. Nothing here is copyrighted or undetermined — every
text is a translation published (or, for the KJV, first issued) well before
1931, mostly Project Gutenberg or LacusCurtius transcriptions of Bohn's
Classical Library, the Loeb Classical Library, or comparable 19th/early-20th-
century editions. Two things were worth a second look and are recorded below:
the Epictetus file is actually a 1948 reprint of an 1865 translation, and the
Hero of Alexandria file's translator credit does not match `metadata.json`.

- `texts/2-rome-late-antiquity/hero-of-alexandria-pneumatics/metadata.json` — `translator` says "Bennet Woodcroft." The source file (PG77400) names the translator as "Joseph George Greenwood" and Woodcroft as the editor of the 1851 volume.
