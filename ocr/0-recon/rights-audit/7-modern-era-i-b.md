# Rights audit — 7-modern-era-i, second half (hegel-philosophy-of-history through woolf-mrs-dalloway)

Audited all 25 texts in the slice; opened the actual source PDF (and, for Kafka, the EPUB's
`content.opf`) for every one of them — no verdict here rests on `metadata.json` alone. Three
surprises: Herzl's file is not the 1896 d'Avigdor translation the metadata claims but a 1946
Alkow revision with Dover apparatus stitched on; Kafka's Project Gutenberg file is explicitly
flagged "COPYRIGHTED" in both the PDF front matter and the EPUB's `dc:rights` (translator David
Wyllie retained his copyright — PG hosts it with permission, we cannot republish it); and
Marinetti's uncredited translation turns out to be the 1973 Viking Press wording (Flint et al.),
not a period translation. Three texts carry an explicit "renewed"/"copyright" notice on a
pre-1931 work (Toomer 1923/renewed 1951, Woolf 1925/renewed by Leonard Woolf) — per the charter's
own Weber lesson, the notice is irrelevant once the 95-year term from publication has run, so
both are public domain regardless of what the page says.

## public domain

Hegel, The Philosophy of History, public domain — Sibree translation, printed in "The World's Great Classics," Colonial Press, copyright 1899; translation itself dates to 1857. Well before 1931.
Hegel, Philosophy of Right, public domain — Dyde translation, George Bell & Sons, 1896; title page confirmed, Cornell Library copy notes no known US copyright restrictions.
Hilbert, The Foundations of Geometry, public domain — Townsend translation, "TRANSLATION COPYRIGHTED BY The Open Court Publishing Co. 1902" printed on copyright page.
Hilbert, Mathematical Problems, public domain — Newson translation, published in Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society vol. VIII, 1902.
Hughes, The Weary Blues, public domain — Alfred A. Knopf, 1926, title page dated and confirmed.
Kierkegaard, Selections from the Writings of Kierkegaard, public domain — Hollander translation, University of Texas Bulletin No. 2326, July 8 1923.
Lovelace and Menabrea, Sketch of the Analytical Engine, public domain — "Scientific Memoirs" extract, London, 1843, printed by Richard and John E. Taylor; Lovelace's notes are part of the same 1843 publication.
Minkowski, Space and Time, public domain — the file is a Wikisource export of the classic Perrett & Jeffery translation (from Methuen's 1923 "The Principle of Relativity"); Wikisource's own colophon additionally releases it under CC BY-SA 4.0/GFDL, so it clears on either ground.
Nietzsche, Beyond Good and Evil, public domain — Zimmern translation, reprinted from The Complete Works of Friedrich Nietzsche, 1909–1913.
Nietzsche, On the Genealogy of Morals, public domain — Samuel and Kennedy translation, The Complete Works of Friedrich Nietzsche vol. VIII, T.N. Foulis, 1913.
Planck, The Origin and Development of the Quantum Theory, public domain — Clarke and Silberstein translation of the 1920 Nobel address, Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1922.
Russell, Introduction to Mathematical Philosophy, public domain — George Allen & Unwin, 1919, original English.
Russell, The Problems of Philosophy, public domain — 1912, original English.
Russell and Whitehead, Principia Mathematica vol. 1, public domain — Cambridge University Press, 1910, original English.
Rutherford, The Scattering of α and β Particles by Matter and the Structure of the Atom, public domain — Philosophical Magazine, 1911, original English journal article, not a later anthology reprint.
Thompson, On Growth and Form, public domain — Cambridge University Press, 1917, original English; title page confirmed. (Note: the 1917 first edition is what's in this file, not the substantially revised 1942 second edition — worth flagging since the two differ a great deal and only the 1917 text is clear.)
Toomer, Cane, public domain — Boni & Liveright, copyright 1923; page explicitly also shows "® 1951 BY JEAN TOOMER" (a real renewal), but the 95-year term from the 1923 publication expired regardless, same pattern as the charter's Weber example.
Wittgenstein, Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus, public domain — Ogden translation with Russell's introduction, Kegan Paul / Harcourt Brace, 1922.
Woolf, Mrs Dalloway, public domain — Harcourt, Brace & World, copyright 1925; page shows "RENEWED BY LEONARD WOOLF" but the 95-year term from 1925 has run — same pattern as Toomer above.

## translation needed

Herzl, The Jewish State, translation needed — the work (Der Judenstaat, Vienna 1896) is public domain, and so is the first English translation (Sylvie d'Avigdor, London: Nutt, 1896), but the file on disk is NOT that translation: its own colophon says it is the Dover 1988 reprint of a 1946 American Zionist Emergency Council edition, "based on a revised translation published by the Scopus Publishing Company... 1943... further revised by Jacob M. Alkow" — three layers removed from d'Avigdor, plus a Louis Lipsky introduction, an Alex Bein biography, a bibliography and chronology that would need stripping as non-authorial apparatus regardless. Use the original 1896 d'Avigdor translation (widely available, e.g. the Internet Archive scan of the Nutt edition) instead of this file.
Kafka, The Metamorphosis, translation needed — Die Verwandlung (1915) is public domain, but the David Wyllie translation used here is explicitly marked "*** This is a COPYRIGHTED Project Gutenberg eBook ***" in the PDF front matter, and the EPUB's `content.opf` states `<dc:rights>Copyrighted. Read the copyright notice inside this book for details.</dc:rights>`. PG hosts it with the translator's permission under PG's terms, which is not equivalent to a licence we can redistribute under MIT. Translate fresh from the public-domain German original, or locate a pre-1931 or explicitly-licensed English translation (the first published English translation was 1933, Muir; also not clear).
Marinetti, The Futurist Manifesto, translation needed — the 1909 Italian original is public domain, but no translator is credited in the file and the wording ("beneath mosque lamps whose brass cupolas are bright as our souls...") matches the 1973 translation by Robert Brain, R.W. Flint, J.C. Higgitt and Caroline Tisdall, published in Apollonio's "Futurist Manifestos" (Viking Press, New York) — solidly in copyright. Translate fresh from the Italian "Fondazione e Manifesto del Futurismo" (Le Figaro, 1909, public domain) or locate an older public-domain rendering.
Saussure, Course in General Linguistics, translation needed — the French "Cours de linguistique générale" (1916, Bally/Sechehaye/Riedlinger eds.) is public domain, but the file is explicitly "COPYRIGHT, 1959, BY THE PHILOSOPHICAL LIBRARY, INC." (Wade Baskin translation) — well within copyright. Translate fresh from the 1916 French edition.

## copyrighted

Schrödinger, What Is Life?, copyrighted — "First published 1944," Cambridge University Press, based on 1943 Dublin lectures; written directly in English so there is no separable translation to fall back on. Not eligible until 2040 (95 years from 1944) absent permission.

## undetermined

Jabotinsky, The Iron Wall, undetermined — the Russian original ("O Zheleznoi Stene," Rassvet, Paris, 4 Nov 1923) is public domain, but the file's actual English text is NOT dated to 1923: per the filename ("...sajr.pdf" = SA Jewish Report) and the file's own sourcing note (jabotinsky.org / marxists.de), the main essay is the English translation that first appeared in the Jewish Herald (South Africa), 26 November 1937, and the file also appends a second, distinct piece, "The Ethics of the Iron Wall," from The Jewish Standard (London), 5 September 1941 — neither translator is credited anywhere in the document. metadata.json's "translator: Lenni Brenner, 1937" appears to conflate the 1937 first-publication date with Brenner, who by outside sources only transcribed/revised these already-existing translations decades later for his 1984 anthology; he did not originate them. What would settle it: identify who actually translated the 1937 Jewish Herald and 1941 Jewish Standard texts, and whether either periodical's US copyright was ever secured/renewed (obscure periodicals of that era very often were not, but that has to be checked, not assumed).

## Metadata corrections

- `texts/7-modern-era-i/herzl-the-jewish-state/metadata.json` — translator field says "Sylvie d'Avigdor", year_translated 1896. The source PDF's own colophon says the text is a 1946 revision by Jacob M. Alkow (based on a 1943 Scopus revision, in turn based on d'Avigdor 1896) — three translation layers removed from what the metadata claims. The file also carries a Louis Lipsky introduction, Alex Bein biography, bibliography and chronology not reflected in metadata.
- `texts/7-modern-era-i/jabotinsky-the-iron-wall/metadata.json` — translator field says "Lenni Brenner", year_translated 1937. Brenner (b. 1937) cannot have translated a text in his birth year; 1937 is the Jewish Herald first-publication date of an anonymous translation Brenner later transcribed/revised, not his own translation date. The file also silently includes a second essay ("The Ethics of the Iron Wall," 1941) not named in metadata at all.
- `texts/7-modern-era-i/marinetti-futurist-manifesto/metadata.json` — translator field is `null`, but the file's wording matches a specific, identifiable, still-copyrighted 1973 translation (Brain/Flint/Higgitt/Tisdall); it should not be left blank.
