# Rights audit — §8 Modern Era II, slice A (amdahl → mccarthy)

Audited all 25 texts in this slice; opened the source file itself (PDF text
layer, PDF metadata, or HTML) for 24 of them — `arendt-eichmann-in-jerusalem`
is a scanned PDF with no text layer on its early pages, so its verdict rests
on the venue triage already recorded in `RIGHTS.md` rather than a page I could
read directly. Two findings worth flagging up front: `karp-reducibility-*`
does not contain Karp's 1972 paper at all — its PDF is a 2000-era course
slide deck by a TA (Chaitanya Swamy) about the paper, so there is no primary
source to judge here. And `church-calculi-of-lambda-conversion` looked like a
plausible non-renewal candidate (Kraus reprint, 1965) but Wikisource's
renewal-database citation settles it the other way: Renewal R452691, in
copyright until 2037. `mccarthy-recursive-functions-of-symbolic-expressions`
resolved the same way — the Online Books Page's CACM copyright-renewal index
explicitly lists April 1960 (v.3 no.4) as a renewed issue, so this one is not
the Shannon situation despite looking identical on the surface.

## public domain

Buchanan, Poetry and Mathematics, public domain — already VERIFIED in
`RIGHTS.md` and confirmed again here: copyright page reads "COPYRIGHT, 1929,
BY SCOTT BUCHANAN / FIRST PRINTING, JULY, 1929", The John Day Company. 1929
first printing, published before 1931.

## translation needed

Gödel, On Formally Undecidable Propositions of Principia Mathematica, translation needed — the file is Meltzer's 1962 translation (Dover, still in print, Meltzer d. 2008), in copyright; the German original (Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik, 1931) clears the 95-year US term on 1 January 2027, which is close but not yet reached. No free English translation exists (see this directory's own `SOURCING.md`: Hirzel 2000 is free but covers only half the paper and drops the notation). Once the original clears, an Enchiridion translation from the German is the path, not any existing English edition.

## undetermined

Church, An Unsolvable Problem of Elementary Number Theory, undetermined — American Journal of Mathematics vol. 58 (Apr. 1936), Johns Hopkins UP. The Online Books Page's serial renewal index says "No issue or contribution copyright renewals were found for this serial," but that same project caveats that only the first active renewal per periodical is guaranteed shown and un-renewed/late-filed periodicals may be omitted — the identical shape of caveat that keeps Shannon's BSTJ paper undetermined elsewhere in this era. Settling it needs a direct search of the Catalog of Copyright Entries renewal volumes for 1963–64 (28 years after 1936), not a serial-level summary.

## no source

Karp, Reducibility Among Combinatorial Problems, no source — the PDF in this directory (`Reducibility_Among_Combinatorial_Problems.pdf`) is not Karp's 1972 paper. Its own metadata names the file "cs722", author "swamy", created with PowerPoint in 2000: it is a course slide deck about the paper, not the paper. The actual 1972 paper was published in *Complexity of Computer Computations* (R. E. Miller and J. W. Thatcher, eds.), Plenum Press, now Springer-held — presumably still in copyright, but that has not been checked because there is nothing here to check yet; someone needs to source the actual paper before a rights verdict is possible.

## copyrighted

Amdahl, Validity of the Single Processor Approach, copyrighted — AFIPS '67 Spring Joint Computer Conference; the PDF is a current ACM Digital Library printout (DOI 10.1145/1465482.1465560) showing live citation/download counts, i.e. ACM is actively distributing and asserting rights today.
Arendt, Eichmann in Jerusalem, copyrighted — Viking 1963, per `RIGHTS.md`'s prior finding (in print, no scan text layer available to re-verify here; not a new check).
Backus, Can Programming Be Liberated from the von Neumann Style?, copyrighted — 1978 ACM Turing Award Lecture, published in Communications of the ACM; post-1963 term, ACM-held.
Berners-Lee, Information Management: A Proposal, copyrighted — the file itself states "Tim Berners-Lee 1989, 1990, 1996, 1998. All rights reserved." No CERN or W3C open release applies to this document; the notice is explicit and current.
Brin and Page, The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine, copyrighted — this copy is Brin and Page's own Stanford InfoLab page (`infolab.stanford.edu/~backrub/google.html`), which carries no copyright or licence statement at all, so its free hosting is evidence of the authors' wishes, not a licence (per the charter's own warning about personal-page copies). The paper was formally published in *Computer Networks and ISDN Systems* 30 (1998) 107–117, an Elsevier journal, which holds the actual copyright.
Cerf and Kahn, A Protocol for Packet Network Intercommunication, copyrighted — every page carries "© 1974 IEEE. Reprinted, with permission, from IEEE Trans on Comms."
Church, The Calculi of Lambda-Conversion, copyrighted — Princeton UP 1941 (Annals of Mathematics Studies No. 6); Wikisource's Stanford Copyright Renewal Database citation gives Renewal R452691, in copyright in the US until 2037. The Kraus 1965 reprint in this directory does not change that.
Codd, A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks, copyrighted — Communications of the ACM, 1970; ACM-held, post-1963 term.
Cook, The Complexity of Theorem-Proving Procedures, copyrighted — STOC '71, current ACM Digital Library printout (DOI 10.1145/800157.805047).
Diffie and Hellman, New Directions in Cryptography, copyrighted — IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, Nov. 1976; IEEE-held, term not close to running out.
Dijkstra, Go To Statement Considered Harmful, copyrighted — the file is the UT Austin Dijkstra Archive transcription of EWD215, whose own footer explains the published form appeared as "Go To Statement Considered Harmful" in Comm. ACM 11 (March 1968), 147–148; ACM-held, no licence on the archive page.
Dijkstra, Notes on Structured Programming, copyrighted — Academic Press 1972 (in *Structured Programming*, Dahl/Dijkstra/Hoare), per `RIGHTS.md`'s prior finding; still in print.
Fanon, The Wretched of the Earth, copyrighted — both the original (Les Damnés de la Terre, 1961, Fanon d. 1961) and this file's translation (Richard Philcox, 2004, Grove Press) are in copyright; not a translation-only problem, per `RIGHTS.md`'s prior finding.
Franklin and Gosling, Molecular Configuration in Sodium Thymonucleate, copyrighted — Nature, 25 April 1953, the same issue as `wilkins-stokes-wilson-*`, which `RIGHTS.md` already VERIFIED carries "© Nature Publishing Group 1953" in its text layer; this companion paper from the same issue is held by the same rights owner.
Hoare, An Axiomatic Basis for Computer Programming, copyrighted — Communications of the ACM, 1969; ACM-held, current ACM Digital Library printout (DOI 10.1145/363235.363259).
Hoare, Communicating Sequential Processes, copyrighted — Communications of the ACM, 1978; ACM-held.
Kubrick and Clarke, 2001: A Space Odyssey (Screenplay), copyrighted — studio-held (per `RIGHTS.md`), and this PDF is an unauthorised fan transcription with no publisher information at all, not an authorised published text.
Lamport, Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System, copyrighted — Communications of the ACM, 1978; ACM-held.
Mankiewicz and Welles, Citizen Kane (Screenplay), copyrighted — studio-held (per `RIGHTS.md`), same unauthorised-transcript situation as the Kubrick/Clarke screenplay.
Martin-Löf, On the Meanings of the Logical Constants and the Justifications of the Logical Laws, copyrighted — 1983 Siena lectures, this typescript dated 1996 carries no licence or public-domain statement anywhere in the file; default is not free absent evidence otherwise.
McCarthy, Recursive Functions of Symbolic Expressions and Their Computation by Machine, Part I, copyrighted — Communications of the ACM, April 1960 (v.3 no.4). The Online Books Page's CACM renewal index explicitly lists this issue among the renewed ones, so — unlike the BSTJ/Shannon case this era is wary of — this is a positive renewal finding, not an absence of evidence.
