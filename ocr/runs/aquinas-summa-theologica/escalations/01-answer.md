Two answers, one for each half of your escalation.

## The printed witness: none is coming, and none is needed

Your diagnosis is right — four PDFs whose producer metadata says
Calibre/Ghostscript and whose pagination reproduces the same Gutenberg
transcription are one witness wearing four coats. Agreement among them
establishes extraction fidelity and nothing about correctness.

**Adopt at `needs-review` and stop there.** No page images of the Benziger
Brothers edition will be supplied. This is a legitimate sourcing limitation
rather than a failure of the run, and `needs-review` is the status that describes
it honestly: machine-processed, not yet read against a printed page.

In `review.md`, say explicitly that no independent witness was consulted and why
the supplied PDFs are not one, so that nobody later reads their agreement as
corroboration.

## Scope: Aquinas's authored, unfinished *Summa*. The file is whole.

Take the first reading. The work ends where Aquinas stopped, at *Tertia Pars*
question 90. **The *Supplementum* is excluded and no further source is to be
acquired for it.**

This follows settled corpus policy rather than a fresh judgment. The *Supplement*
was compiled after Aquinas's death by another hand, assembled out of his earlier
*Commentary on the Sentences* — so it is neither written by him for this work nor
authorial matter of any kind. The corpus met this exact question in Chaucer:
Skeat prints *The Tale of Gamelyn* because it stands in the Harleian manuscript,
and we dropped it, because it is not Chaucer's and shipping it under his name
would be a worse error than an incomplete edition. The *Supplementum* is the same
case, and it gets the same answer.

The unfinished ending is the interesting fact about this work, not a defect in
our copy. Aquinas stopped writing in December 1273 and never resumed.

So: **the proposed file is complete in scope.** In `review.md` state that the
text ends at III q.90, that this is where Aquinas himself stopped, and that the
conventional *Supplementum* is deliberately excluded and why. A reader who knows
the printed editions will otherwise assume our copy is truncated, and the record
should answer that before they ask.

Leave `ocr_status` to the adoption step as usual.
