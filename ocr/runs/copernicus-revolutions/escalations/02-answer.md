and right not to guess — this one sets precedent for every critical edition

**Remove both the editorial labels and the rejected authorial passages. Keep

The reasoning, so you can apply it to the cases I have not seen:

The test is not "are these words the author's?" but "are these words the work?"
Copernicus's deleted drafts are his, and they are still not *De revolutionibus*
— they are the edition's scholarship about how the book came to be. A reader
going straight through would meet superseded wording presented alongside the
finished text, and the fact that our reader renders brackets plainly makes it
worse, not better: the labels look like part of the sentence.

The corpus has met this before and answered the same way. Skeat's edition of
Chaucer prints a band of manuscript collation under every page, and the run that
processed it dropped 1,241 of those blocks as apparatus. Same class: variant
readings recorded by an editor to show what the text might have been.

So the distinction that governs the bracket rule is *whose act of writing the
bracket represents*. A translator's interpolation inside a sentence — "[for
negligence]" — is part of rendering this text into English, and stays. An
editor's label announcing a variant is a statement *about* the text, and goes,
taking its variant with it.

Practically:

1. Remove the labelled passages and the labels together. A label left behind
   with nothing after it is worse than either choice.
2. **Assert the count.** Report how many you removed, broken down by label type
   (`[Earlier draft:]`, `[Printed text:]`, `[Deleted version:]`, `[In the
   autograph ...]`). If a label form appears that is not on that list, stop and
   list it rather than extending the pattern by guess.
3. **Verify no ordinary bracket was caught.** Translator interpolations and
   editorial glosses inside sentences must survive. Run a before/after count of
   all bracketed spans and account for the difference exactly.
4. Record the decision and the counts in `NOTES.md` for `review.md`. A reader
   who knows this edition will notice the apparatus is gone and should find our
   reasoning rather than wonder.

Then finish as planned: verify the 140 images resolve and sit where the text
calls for them, leave doubtful numbers in tables unrepaired and page-indexed for
the reviewer, and propose at `needs-review`.
