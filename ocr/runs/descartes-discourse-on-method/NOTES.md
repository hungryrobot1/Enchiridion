## Pipeline findings and time

- This text is a clean example of why EPUB recon must precede OCR: the source
  already contains the complete prose, and the default PDF round trip would
  add recognition errors without recovering any structure.
- The stage-4 premise does not hold for this source pair. EPUB/PDF agreement is
  exceptionally high yet visibly preserves missing words and punctuation. A
  same-transcription PDF is a fidelity oracle and a correctness non-witness.
- `recon-epub.py` correctly identifies zero notation here. Manual archive/XHTML
  inspection supplied the necessary control that the zero was not caused by
  looking for only one notation convention.
- The supplied metadata demonstrates a validation gap: its era contradicts its
  own date, while its translation year is not evidenced by the supplied file.
  Stage documentation says to verify the title page but does not say how to
  represent a bibliographic field the title page cannot establish.
- Recon, extraction, regeneration, and checks each took seconds. Most time went
  to distinguishing a derivative PDF from an independent witness, reviewing
  internally repairable prose defects, and bounding unresolved readings. That
  is genuinely textual/evidentiary work rather than computational cost.
- The slowest avoidable step was an initial character-level duplicate probe
  with `difflib` autojunk disabled on near-repetitive prose. Switching that
  local duplicate scan to its intended heuristic mode reduced it from over a
  minute to seconds without changing the threshold or result.
