## For the reviewer

The proposed text is `hamilton-madison-jay-federalist-papers.md`, containing the
complete Project Gutenberg 18 transcription of *The Federalist Papers*: Nos.
1–69, both supplied versions of No. 70, and Nos. 71–85. There are 86 paper
sections, 86 bylines, and 69 authorial footnote marker/note pairs. The title,
authors, English language, and 1788 date in `metadata.json` agree with the EPUB
package and the supplied PDF title page. I did not change `ocr_status`.

The EPUB is the structured source. The supplied 261-page PDF was generated by
Calibre 9.5.0 from the same Project Gutenberg transcription. It is useful for
locating a string and seeing the transcription rendered, but it is not an
independent printed witness and cannot establish that Gutenberg read an
eighteenth-century page correctly. No word-by-word comparison against an
independent scan was performed, and no stage-4/page-adjudicated repair was made.

The brief requires both versions of No. 70 to stay. The first is EPUB spine
document 70 (`...18-h-70.htm.xhtml`) and the second is spine document 71
(`...18-h-71.htm.xhtml`); both are headed `THE FEDERALIST. No. LXX.`, carry the
same title, date, and Hamilton byline, and remain separate in the proposal. The
source contains the explanatory transcriber sentence twice: once in parentheses
after the first heading and once, malformed with a leading bare asterisk, after
the first version. The brief mentions only the opening occurrence but directs
that Gutenberg's explanatory parenthetical come out; I treated both copies as
the same transcriber apparatus and removed both. This is the only respect in
which the file contained more of that apparatus than the brief described.

I also removed the electronic contents and the final `Transcriber's Notes:`
cover-art notice. Gutenberg's start/end wrapper was removed by the generic EPUB

## Where this was harder than it needed to be

The route documentation still pulls in two directions. The canonical stage rule
says every structured EPUB is source-native, including prose, while the
`extract-epub.py` docstring opens by describing the tool as being for EPUBs with
recoverable LaTeX. I had to read past the tool's stated audience to establish
that it is also the intended prose extractor.

The source-identity check is corpus-wide and has no single-file argument. Its
controls worked, but I could not point it at this workspace's copied source; I
had to inspect the OPF metadata and PDF title page separately.

The EPUB extractor's `--report` is entirely notation-shaped. It called this run
clean while emitting a malformed bare-asterisk transcriber sentence and while
the prose carried dozens of impossible strings. The zero was truthful about
formulas and nearly content-free for this book.

I expected to need a small text-specific build script, but I did not expect to
need a separate structural verifier merely to assert that all 86 headings,
bylines, signatures, and footnotes survived. The generic extraction acceptance
test is framed around OCR pages and gives no comparable completeness test for a
long prose EPUB.

The ordering placed the suspicious-token review after extraction, which was
necessary, but the duplicate No. 70 made every defect in that paper appear twice
and initially obscured substring counts (`pub lic` also occurs inside `repub
lican`). That made exact-anchor repair ordering matter in a way visible only
when the build first failed.

The ambiguous choice was where stage 3 ended. I repaired unique impossible
spellings, but left concatenated compounds and plausible eighteenth-century
variants because inserting a space versus a hyphen, or modernizing a spelling,
cannot be decided internally. On another day it would have been easy to call
those mechanical cleanup; without an independent page, that would have been a
silent editorial normalization.
