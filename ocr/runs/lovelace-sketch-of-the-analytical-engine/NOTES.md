## For the reviewer

The proposed text is `lovelace-sketch-of-the-analytical-engine.md`. It contains
Menabrea's complete memoir, Lovelace's Notes A–G, and all 30 retained footnotes.
The source-native witness is Project Gutenberg 75107's EPUB: its 1,522 formula
image occurrences carry the LaTeX used to set them in `data-tex` attributes.
The supplied PDF was generated by Calibre from the same transcription. It can
settle layout, including the table spans, but it is not an independent witness
to the correctness of a word, number, or mathematical mark.

The Bernoulli operation diagram should be checked first, on printed pages 44–46
(PDF leaves 45–47). It is preserved as the EPUB's two structural tables, not as
an image: the main table is 33 rows by as many as 21 columns, and the companion
result table is 33 rows by 5 columns. The resolved reader policy keeps all
`rowspan` and `colspan` values in bare HTML. The “diagram at the end of these
Notes” cited by footnotes [23] and [29] is these tables; it is not a missing
illustration asset. This resolves the open diagram question in `BRIEF.md`.

No reading was corrected from the Calibre PDF, because it is another rendering
of the EPUB transcription rather than a printed witness. Check these doubtful
readings against an independent image of the 1843 edition:

- Printed p. 5 / PDF leaf 6 — the source citation says `October 1812`. The work,
  surrounding chronology, and metadata point toward 1842, but the supplied PDF
  merely repeats `1812`, so the proposal retains it.
- Printed pp. 21, 44, 61, and 65 — six formula strings use an em dash where a
  mathematical minus may have been printed: `n — 1`, `n — 2`, `(mn' — m'n`,
  `\overline{n + 2} — 3`, `2n — 3`, and `2n —1`. They render with KaTeX
  warnings and were not normalized without a page witness.
- Printed p. 46 — the prose after the result table says “tabulate
  $\mathrm{B}_1$, $\mathrm{B}_1$, &c.” In context the second subscript may be
  `3`, but that is a value-changing repair and remains open.
- Printed p. 59 — adjacent formulae use both `$e^x$` and `$\epsilon^x$` for the
  exponential base. The vocabulary census correctly surfaces this as an
  `e/epsilon` confusable in Note G. The shared transcription cannot determine
  whether the edition distinguishes them or one was misread.
- Footnote [20], in the footnote gathering beginning on printed p. 48 / PDF
  leaf 49 — the source reads “is given on in” and “his explanation In being”.
  More than one grammatical repair is possible, so both remain unchanged.

Three readings were repaired solely on evidence inside the EPUB, under the
stage-3 rule rather than by claiming a page read:

- `columns w e will designate` became `columns we will designate` once; the
  source line wrap split the English pronoun and no competing reading exists.
- `the n*nature of the principles*` became `the *nature of the principles*`
  once in footnote [18]; the XHTML itself has the impossible sequence
  `the n<i>nature`.
- The link target and id for the Note G marker are both number 29, but its
  visible source text is `[30]`, leaving no `[29]` marker and two `[30]`
  markers. The marker was restored to `[29]` from that internally complete
  1–30 sequence; the separate `[30]` marker is unchanged.

The table heading containing marker [11] displays `[11]` without a superscript
tag because the resolved table whitelist permits only `table`, `tr`, `th`,
`td`, `rowspan`, and `colspan`. The marker remains present and unambiguous; the
other 29 body markers are non-navigating `<sup>` elements.

## Scope and editorial decisions

The extraction walks EPUB content documents 0–4 in numeric order. Document 0's
Gutenberg header is removed structurally while its title page stays. Documents
1–4 supply the memoir, Notes A–G, and footnotes 1–30. Document 5, the Project
Gutenberg transcriber's note, and document 6, the licence, are excluded.

The long bracketed block before Menabrea's opening ends with the explicit
signature `—EDITOR.]` and is largely an editorial bibliography. It was removed
as apparatus despite sharing content file 1 with the memoir. That cut also
removed 14 formula images quoted in the editor's prefatory material. Lovelace's
Notes A–G, both `A.A.L.` signatures, and every numbered footnote were retained
as the brief requires.

At 226 KB the text is above the reader's eager-parsing threshold. The title is
the opening `h1`; `ARTICLE XXIX.` and `NOTES BY THE TRANSLATOR.` are promoted to
the two subsequent `h1` divisions. Notes A–G and each retained footnote group
nest at `h2`.

## Table decision and counts

The EPUB contains exactly 18 tables: 4 in content file 1, 10 in file 2, and 4
in file 3. Thirteen use at least one `rowspan` or `colspan`; those 13 are emitted
as bare HTML tables. Five are rectangular and span-free; those five are emitted
as valid Markdown pipe tables with separator rows.

For every HTML table, the output retains only `table`, `tr`, `th`, `td`,
`rowspan`, and `colspan`. Classes, ids, styles, alignment, widths, and section
wrappers are absent. The verifier compares the complete source/output span-value
census, direct geometry, and all 800 formula strings in the 13 HTML tables. The
two Note G tables verify as 33×21 and 33×5. No wrapper div, width hint, class, or
inline style was added; the reader owns horizontal scrolling.

## Acceptance and limits

`verify-lovelace.py` reported:

```text
verified: 5 work documents; 18 tables = 13 minimal HTML + 5 Markdown; Note G 33x21 and 33x5; notes 1-30 retained; editor/PG apparatus absent
```

The controlled diagnostic triad first made each checker reject a planted defect
and then passed the proposal. Direct KaTeX checking found zero failures across
1,508 math blocks. The check also produced 37 non-fatal strict-mode warnings in
19 blocks: 29 warnings from 11 `array` formulae whose source declares fewer
columns than its alignment marks use, six warnings from the em-dash formulae
listed above, and two terminal-linebreak warnings. These are recoverable
`data-tex` strings, render successfully, and were retained rather than silently
rewritten. Array-warning formulae occur on printed pp. 43, 46–47, 54, 63–64,
and 66, plus three formulae before the first page marker in content file 2.

The math vocabulary census saw 1,508 spans, 2,807 command uses, and 36 distinct
commands. It found no STRAYS, no foreign script in the examined spans, and no
same-formula Latin/Greek confusable. Its FLAT reports mix ordinary operators,
ellipsis, cosine, and theta from heterogeneous tables rather than identifying a
single damaged glyph. The one section-level confusable, `e/epsilon` on p. 59,
is left for the reviewer as noted above. Twelve spans longer than 300 characters
were deliberately skipped by the census; the linter and KaTeX consumer both
accepted their delimiters and syntax, but that does not establish their meaning.

Additional audits found no Gutenberg markers, transcriber's note, editor block,
in-page anchors, code fences, recoverability comments, replacement characters,
undecoded common entities, or Cyrillic/Han/Arabic/Hebrew characters. The source
PDF's agreement with the EPUB was not counted as corroboration. With no
independent printed witness, the proposal belongs at `needs-review`; `ocr_status`
was not changed.

## Time and difficulty

Most of the work went into preserving and verifying the 18 tables. That was
genuinely intricate because the meaning is carried jointly by merged headers,
long row groups, formula images, and cells extending across physical page turns.
The avoidable time went into discovering that a green notation report and green
renderer checks said nothing about table structure, then building a second
structural account of the EPUB.

Localizing KaTeX warnings was also tooling-heavy. The standard checker exits
successfully and writes warning text without source locations, so the difference
between a harmless warning family and one damaged formula was not visible from
its result alone.

## Where this was harder than it needed to be

The source-route argument is repeated at length in the task charter, top-level
README, recon contract, and extraction contract. I had to read all four because
the boundaries between general rule, rationale, and possible exception were not
obvious. Stage 1 is almost entirely PDF-oriented and never plainly says that a
source-native EPUB has no prepared derivative.

I had to build `audit-epub-tables.py`, `extract-lovelace.py`, and
`verify-lovelace.py`. The generic extractor's anomaly report covers notation
strings only, but its clean headline appeared after it had silently discarded
13 tables' spans and emitted all 18 tables without valid Markdown separators.
Nothing in recon reported `rowspan` or `colspan`, even though those attributes
decided whether the work's most important object survived.

The ordering fought the run because table geometry was cheapest to inspect at
recon. It surfaced only after generic extraction and controlled renderer checks
had both passed. The diagram question likewise looked like an image-inventory
question until late source inspection showed that it was XHTML structure made
partly from formula SVGs.

Three choices were ambiguous enough to require judgment: treating the
explicitly signed editor block as apparatus despite the brief's coarse “keep
file 1” disposition; promoting the two major divisions to `h1` because of file
length; and retaining warning-producing source LaTeX rather than normalizing
array specifications without a printed witness. The resolved HTML-table ruling
removed the largest ambiguity and determined the final representation.
