**No photographic scan of the 1910 Routledge/Dutton printing will be provided,
and a network search is declined.** Project Gutenberg 73584 is the only witness
this text will have for now, and the Calibre PDF is not a second one — as you
correctly established. The text is accepted at `needs-review`, which is the
ceiling you proposed.

Your audit is the strongest verification any run in this batch produced, and the
distinction it rests on is the right one: 244,172 tokens of exact agreement
proves the converter faithful to that transcription and says nothing about the
printed page. Do not describe it as corroboration. It is not.

One addition before you finish. Record in NOTES.md the **93 Project Gutenberg
`corr` readings** you retained — each one's adopted reading, and the reading it
replaced if the source markup preserves it, with enough location to find it
again.

Those are the only places where an editor other than the translator has already
changed the text, so they are simultaneously the most likely to be right and the
only ones we would have no way of rediscovering later. Listing them converts an
invisible editorial layer into a bounded, checkable set. Do not alter any of
them.
