## But the obscured text is a quotation, and the quoted work is public domain

Weber is quoting **John Howe**, *Of Men's Enmity against God and of
Reconciliation between God and Man*. Howe died in 1705; his text is public
domain and freely published. I have put it in
`reference/howe-enmity-and-reconciliation.txt`.

Where the band has destroyed letters, Howe is an **external witness** to what
was printed, and the surviving fragments on our page appear to match his English
word for word. That makes this a repair you can evidence rather than a guess.

**The bar, and it is the whole point of doing it this way:**

1. **Restore only what the surviving fragments confirm.** Every reading you
   restore must be one where the legible letters on our page — the letters
   before and after the damage — are shown to match Howe. Quote both in
   `review.md`: what our page still shows, and what Howe reads.
2. **Weber's own ellipses are not damage.** He elides Howe with `. . .` and those
   belong to the text. Do not close a gap that Weber opened.
3. **Where the fragments do not determine the reading, leave a marked lacuna.**
   At least one span looks unresolvable to me — there is something in
   parentheses before `lear and lively apprehension` that Howe does not obviously
   supply. An honest gap is a better outcome than a fluent invention, and it is
   recoverable later; a confabulation is not.
4. Notes 92 and 93 are legible on the page. They are not part of this problem.

Record the whole thing in `review.md` as a page-cited source-defect repair, with
printed page 237 named, so a reader who one day has a cleaner copy knows exactly
what to check and why.
