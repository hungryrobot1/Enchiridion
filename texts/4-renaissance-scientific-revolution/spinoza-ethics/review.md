### For the reviewer

`spinoza-ethics.md` is the complete five-part *Ethics* in R. H. M. Elwes's
translation: 87,561 words, extracted from `pg3800-images-3.epub`. The supplied
113-page `pg3800-images-3.pdf` was generated by Calibre/Ghostscript from the
same Project Gutenberg transcription. An independent PDF-native extraction and
the EPUB extraction agree exactly across the entire 108,535-token work stream
after removing standalone PDF page numbers and Markdown syntax. This is strong
evidence that the conversion lost or altered nothing. It is **not** evidence
that Gutenberg's transcription is correct: EPUB and PDF are two renderings of
one act of copying.

There is no independent printed witness in `source/`, so no whole-work stage-4
proofreading claim is made. The best first review step is to obtain a page scan
of the Elwes edition represented here and compare the text against it. The
supplied PDF can settle layout and note placement, but not a reading shared with
the EPUB.

Page-indexed decisions and repairs (PDF file pages, one-indexed):

- Page 53: note 6, “By ‘men’ in this and the following propositions …”, was
  retained as authorial. Its first-person voice defines Spinoza's own term and
  the note is printed beside Proposition XXIX. The body marker is a non-linking
  superscript.
- Page 59: note 10, “This is possible, though the human mind …”, was retained
  as authorial. It speaks in Spinoza's first person and cites his own II. xiii.
  note. The body marker is a non-linking superscript.
- Page 28: `My intention her was only` was repaired to `My intention here was
  only` under stage-3 internal-evidence licence: the source string is impossible
  English in context and has exactly one grammatical repair.
- Page 89: `signifieded by Moses` was repaired to `signified by Moses` under
  the same licence.
- Page 91: `What have said in this Part` was repaired to `What I have said in
  this Part` under the same licence.

The other numbered notes—1–5, 7–9, and 11–17—were removed with their body
markers as translator/editor apparatus. They gloss Latin choices, cite Pollock,
Van Vloten, Bruder, Camerer, or Land, identify quotations, or discuss variant
readings. Notes 6 and 10 were the only notes whose contents identify them as
Spinoza's voice. The EPUB and PDF also repeat all seventeen notes as an endnote
gathering after “End of the Ethics” (PDF pages 106–108); that duplicate gathering
was removed while the two authorial notes were kept at their in-text positions.

No other specific doubtful reading was adjudicated. Archaic spellings and
unusual wording were left untouched because the supplied sources cannot decide
whether they reproduce Elwes accurately.

### Extraction and post-processing

The repository's `2-extract/extract-epub.py --report` produced
`spinoza-ethics.raw.md`: 87,997 words, zero formulas, zero illustrations, and no
notation anomalies across zero formulas. The zero is descriptive, not a
meaningful notation check; this work contains no mathematics.

`build_spinoza.py` performs every change with asserted anchors and counts. It:

- removes the five-cell title-page contents table;
- removes the post-finis duplicate endnote gathering;
- removes fifteen translator/editor notes and their body markers;
- retains authorial notes 6 and 10 with non-navigating superscript markers;
- promotes all five Parts to `h1` after the document title and normalizes their
  subdivisions to `h2`, so the reader's lazy sectioning starts at Part I;
- rejoins source-code line wrapping within existing EPUB block boundaries; and
- makes the three internally licensed syntax repairs listed for the reviewer.

The title, Latin subtitle, author, and translator credit remain. The complete
work begins at Part I and ends with the final paragraph of Part V. Gutenberg
license matter, the miniature contents table, the finis line, editorial notes,
and duplicate endnotes are edition furniture and are absent.

### Verification

`verify_spinoza.py` reruns the official EPUB extractor and requires the raw
output to match byte-for-byte, reruns the build and requires the proposal to
match byte-for-byte, checks heading and apparatus invariants, and independently
extracts the supplied PDF. The complete EPUB and PDF work streams matched
exactly across 108,535 normalized tokens. Again, this proves fidelity between
two renderings of the same transcription, not correctness.

The controlled diagnostic triad first rejected a planted defect for each of
`lint-math.py`, `check-math.js`, and `check-raw-latex.js`, then passed the final
text. It scanned zero math blocks. This says the reader sees no malformed math
or raw LaTeX; it says nothing about Spinoza's words.

Additional audits found:

### Where this was harder than it needed to be

The route rule was easy to find in `2-extract/STAGE.md`, but its result conflicts
with the extractor's own framing: recon explicitly says a prose EPUB should be
extracted directly, while `extract-epub.py` says it is for EPUBs with recoverable
LaTeX. I had to read the extractor implementation to learn that it also handles
plain prose safely. The apparatus decision rules live in the long top-level
README rather than beside the stage-3 mechanics, so note classification required
moving back and forth between two documents.

I expected a generic EPUB-prose finalizer that preserved block boundaries while
rejoining source-code wraps and asserting furniture cuts. There was none. The
closest precedent was embedded in Mill's text-specific builder, so I had to
write `build_spinoza.py`. I also expected an existing exact EPUB-versus-rendered-
PDF stream comparison; `verify_spinoza.py` had to supply it.

The apparatus detector was cheap but badly ordered for this run. I ran it after
building the final text; its 578-entry long-prose dump was mostly noise, but the
dump incidentally exposed three broken strings that then required another build
and full verification pass. The high-confidence result was useful; the review
section was too large to inspect as a report and was truncated in the terminal.

The ambiguous choices I resolved were the authorship of notes 6 and 10, the
classification of the other fifteen notes as translation/editorial apparatus,
and whether the irregular EPUB heading levels represented real hierarchy. I
used first-person internal references for the notes and the work's explicit
five-Part structure for headings. Another run could reasonably have preserved
the Latin subtitle as a heading instead of title-page italics; nothing in the
stage contract settles title-page typography below the first `h1`.
