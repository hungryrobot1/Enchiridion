### Where this was harder than it needed to be

The routing rule is spread across the OCR README, stage 0, and stage 2. Even
though the README names stage 2 as canonical, all three had to be read to learn
what evidence each tool expected and what an `UNDETERMINED` PDF verdict meant
once a decisive EPUB was already present. The extraction tool's description is
dominated by notation recovery, which made its use for a no-image prose EPUB
less obvious than the route verdict itself.

The brief's “header, licence, and nothing else” instruction directly conflicts
with the stage-3 rule to remove edition contents and editorial notes. The brief
is binding, so the result retains material the general policy says to strip.
This was not a subtle classification problem; it was two authoritative
instructions pointing in opposite directions.

I had to build a text-specific prose-EPUB builder and a PDF-token fidelity
verifier. The repository has several author-specific versions of both patterns
but no generic prose-EPUB reflower or shared-transcription comparator that can
be configured with asserted boundaries.

Ordering fought the work twice. An exact census of `Sect. N.` initially looked
like sections 10 and 219 were missing; only later inspection showed that the
paragraphs were present under `Sect,` and `Sec.`. The syntactically broken Sect.
2 sentence surfaced during final visual reading, after structural processing
and fidelity comparison had already passed, because both supplied formats carry
the same loss.

The choices I could reasonably have made differently were how to interpret
“Gutenberg header,” whether the standalone `Book II` leaf should become reader
structure or merely remain visible, and how to nest chapter headings beneath
it. I removed only the explicit digitization paragraph, retained `Book II` as a
visible pre-chapter heading, and made each chapter the lazy top-level section so
the 300 KB work does not sit in one eager reader section.
