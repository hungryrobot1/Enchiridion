### For the reviewer

The proposed file is `douglass-narrative-of-the-life-of-frederick-douglass.md`.
The primary source is Project Gutenberg EPUB 23, extracted source-natively. Its
sibling PDF was generated by Calibre 9.5.0 from the same Gutenberg
transcription. Agreement between them establishes extraction fidelity, not
correctness; the supplied files contain no independent printed witness.

Phillips's letter, Chapters I–XI in sequence, and Douglass's Appendix including

with their superscript markers but without in-page navigation.

structural changes: it reflows source-code line wrapping, restores five XHTML
poem blocks and three non-poem `<br>` groups as Markdown hard breaks, separates
Garrison's signature from the following `BOSTON,` location, promotes major

literal `<sup>` markers. Those changes are asserted in `build_douglass.py`.

Check these readings first against an independent image of the 1845 printing:

### Route and scope

`recon-epub.py` reported 19 spine documents, `h1×1 h2×19 h4×1 h5×1`, no `<img>`
elements, no notation, and `ROUTE: source-native`. Direct archive inspection
found a convention the recon tool explicitly does not count: `wrap0000.xhtml`
uses SVG `<image>` to reference a 94,983-byte PNG cover. The brief's bare “no
images” assertion is therefore literally wrong. The image is only the cover,
not notation or textual content, so visual inspection does not change the
source-native route. Stage 1 splitting, cropping, and duplicate-leaf scanning
do not apply because the PDF is not the extraction input.

The generic extractor reported 40,810 words, one Markdown table, zero formulas,
zero illustrations, and no anomalies. Its notation result is vacuous for this
prose text. The reader build contains 40,414 words.

The brief says “Out: only the PG header and licence,” but that conflicts with
the stage-3 apparatus contract and with evidence inside the file. I followed
the STAGE as instructed. In addition to Gutenberg's automatically trimmed
header and licence, the build removes:

- the electronic-release note honoring Martin Luther King Jr.;
- the edition contents table;
- the administrative Boston publisher/imprint and copyright-registration
  lines; and
- the entire modern `FREDERICK DOUGLASS.` biography spine document, which
  mentions the Civil War, later autobiographies, and Douglass's death in 1895
  and therefore cannot belong to the 1845 edition.

The last item is also a specific defect in the brief: it inventories that leaf
without attributing or dating it, then implies that it stays. The file provides
decisive contrary evidence, and the stage contract removes editorial matter.
The text-specific brief decision retaining Garrison and Phillips is narrower
and evidence-backed, so those pieces stay as directed. Douglass's Appendix is
authorial and stays independently of the exception.

### Where this was harder than it needed to be

The route documentation was too thick for this simple prose EPUB. The decisive
rule is short, but the main README, stage 0, stage 2, the task charter, and the
brief all repeat qualifications around it. The shared extractor's report then
devotes most of its output to notation even when it has counted zero formulas;
the operative prose warning is buried below a vacuous clean result.

The brief was the most costly ambiguity. Its “no images” statement is false
because recon ignores SVG `<image>`, and its “only” removal instruction silently
retains an unmistakably modern biography. Establishing that required opening
the archive after both the brief and recon had sounded conclusive. The brief's
well-evidenced Garrison/Phillips decision was useful; its bare inventory and
scope assertion were not.

I had to build a text-specific prose-EPUB builder. I expected the shared
source-native extractor to preserve ordinary XHTML paragraph and `<br>`
boundaries. Instead it concatenated `WM. LLOYD GARRISON` with `BOSTON,` and
turned every verse line into a separate paragraph. Word-level completeness
passes both defects perfectly, so they surfaced only through XHTML inspection.

The ordering fought the work because structural fidelity was checked only after
extraction and scope decisions. The recon verdict missed the SVG cover, and the
completeness check cannot see flattened layout. Both discoveries came after the
tools most naturally associated with those questions had already returned
clean results.

The choices I could reasonably have made differently were whether the title-page
imprint counted as historical title matter or administrative publisher matter,
how conspicuously to attribute the retained Garrison and Phillips pieces, and
whether suspicious but source-consistent `they,v` and `dona` were internally
repairable. I followed the stage's publisher-matter rule, used short italic
attribution lines, and left both doubtful readings for an independent printed
witness.
