### For the reviewer

This is a source-native extraction from a 2026 Wikisource EPUB of Einstein's
1916 paper in the Perrett and Jeffery translation. The EPUB carries 571
recoverable LaTeX strings in `mediawiki-alt`; the supplied PDF was generated by
Calibre from the same transcription. Agreement between them establishes
fidelity to that transcription, not correctness against the 1923 print edition.

The proposed text contains all 571 formula strings in source order, sixteen
authorial notes, and the light-ray diagram. I removed only the Wikisource export
title card, license material, and “About this digital edition” footer. The
generic extractor corrupted `\sigma` in three formulas in notes 8 and 11 because
its list whitespace normalization consumed the `\s` of the command. I restored
the EPUB's exact strings by asserted anchors; the rendered PDF page 33 also
shows sigma in all three positions. I also removed the duplicated prose `g`
before `$g_{\mu \nu}$` on PDF page 5: the XHTML itself says `system g` followed
immediately by a formula beginning with `g`, so the defect and its single repair
are internal to the document rather than a choice about a printed reading.

Check these unresolved readings first. Each is internally suspicious, but I did
not change it because the supplied PDF renders the same transcription and is
not an independent printed witness:

- PDF p. 18, equation (41): denominator `\partial z_{\sigma}` where the entire
  surrounding derivation differentiates with respect to `x`.
- PDF p. 22, equation (49): `\int d_{\sigma}^{4}dV` beside the consistently used
  energy components `t_{\sigma}^{\alpha}`.
- PDF p. 26, equation (58a): both metric factors carry first index `\mu` in an
  expression for `T_{\mu\nu}`; the second may or may not be `\nu` in print.
- PDF p. 26, equation (60): the first and third denominators both end in
  `x_{\varrho}` although the displayed identity is cyclic.
- PDF p. 27, equation (62): `g^{\mu\alpha}q^{\nu\beta}` uses a lone `q` where a
  second fundamental-tensor factor might be expected.

The math-vocabulary census found no foreign-script characters, shattered-glyph
families, kind strays, or dominant-command trespassers. Its Latin/Greek
confusable reports (`t`/`\tau`, `x`/`\chi`, and similar pairs) are ordinary
variables and indices used together, not evidence for a repair. Fourteen very
long formula spans were skipped by that census's foreign-script check; the
controlled delimiter linter and KaTeX consumer both accept them.

### Where this was harder than it needed to be

The documentation was too thick around routing and the meaning of clean checks.
The same cautions had to be read in the 424-line README, the task charter, the
brief, and several stage contracts before the one operative fact was clear. The
source-native route itself was simple; establishing which repeated warnings
were authoritative took longer than running it.

I had to build `verify_source_fidelity.py`, an ordered formula-string comparator
that I expected the EPUB route to already have. Count equality and the
extractor's clean anomaly report both missed three corrupted `\sigma` commands.
The existing report examines the strings it has already produced, so it cannot
notice damage introduced while those strings pass through later rendering
branches.

The ordering fought the work in two places. The strict source comparison came
after the count and anomaly report had already looked conclusive; doing the
comparison first would have exposed the list-normalization defect immediately.
And the display-context count changed from 112 in raw extraction to 111 after
apparatus removal, so an assertion derived before the cut was wrong even though
the rule was right.

The generic extractor's description of context promotion was ambiguous in a
consequential way. It promotes a sole formula only for a small set of element
types, while formula-only nested `div` containers take a different branch and
remain inline. The report said “104 display” but did not report the 111
standalone inline blocks left behind, even though this text's brief exists
because that same container shape previously lost formulas altogether.

I chose to use the source's British title spelling, `GENERALISED`, rather than
the metadata's `GENERAL`, because the source title is what the text actually
prints. I also classified the light-ray image as work and the five other unique
images as Wikisource furniture. Both choices were straightforward once the raw
markup and rendered pages were open, but neither follows from image counts or
metadata alone.
