# Adjudication — pass 1 against pass 2

Two translators, neither having seen the other's work: pass 1 by hand, pass 2
by Codex (gpt-5.6-sol) from the French and the ledger alone. Word-level
similarity per prose segment ran **0.79 to 0.93**; the two apparent outliers
were artefacts (seg:4 is sixteen words; seg:10's score was footnote
contamination, 0.67 once stripped). Both passes clear `check-pass.py`.

**The passes never disagree about meaning.** Every divergence but one is
synonymic — calculation/reckoning, manner/fashion, remote/far-off, as/by. The
one exception falls on the witness's one doubtful reading, which is exactly
what a two-pass control is for.

## Decisions, and why

- **calcul → calculation** (pass 2). The cognate. Pass 1's "reckoning" was a
  departure it never recorded; pass 2 caught it by simply following policy.
  Also *manière de calcul* → "manner of calculation", not "calculus", which
  would import Leibniz's own later technical sense.
- **s'écrit par 10 → written as 10** (pass 2). "By" is the word-for-word
  rendering and misleads: the numeral is the form the number takes, not the
  means. Plain in syntax.
- **inclusivement → inclusively** (pass 1). Cognate.
- **je ne recommande point → I do not at all recommend** (pass 1). *Point* is
  the emphatic negation; dropping "at all" softens him.
- **nombres figurés → figurate numbers** (pass 2). Cognate, and the standard
  English term.
- **plus d'un millenaire d'années → more than a millennium of years** (pass
  2). Leibniz's pleonasm, kept — cognate-first preserves the author's redundancy
  rather than tidying it.
- **R. P. Bouvet stays R. P.** (pass 1, per ledger). Pass 2 went to "Rev. Fr."
  without recording it; the ledger holds.
- **la Table d'une fois un est un → the Table of once one is one** (pass 1).
  The traditional English name of the multiplication table.
- **reviennent toutes à → all come back to** (pass 1). Closer to the word; "reduce
  to" would be idiomatic and interpretive.
- **seg:4** pass 2's sentence shape adopted: it flows and loses nothing.
- **Title:** *sur ce qu'elle donne le sens* → "on its giving the meaning of";
  pass 1's "what it gives of the meaning" was faithful and unreadable, pass 2's
  "how it gives" smoothed a nuance away. Gerhardt's item number "XXI." is
  editorial and dropped.
- **marvellous → marvelous.** House spelling, American.

## The one semantic divergence

seg:9, *ne l'étoit pas tout dans ces tems éloignés*. Pass 1 translated the
expected idiom ("was not so at all"), footnoting that Gerhardt prints *pas
tout*. Pass 2 translated the printed words ("was not entirely so"). **Pass 2 is
right by the charter** — translate what is printed — and the footnote now
carries both: the printing is confirmed by three witnesses, the idiom *pas du
tout* is likelier, the 1705 Mémoires can settle it someday. That this was the
only place the passes parted is the strongest thing this pilot has shown: the
method finds the genuinely uncertain sentence and nothing else.

## What pass 2 reported

Four departures from cognate, three already in the ledger and one — *consentement
→ agreement* — that pass 1 had also made unprompted; recorded as independently
reached. No transcription concerns. No ledger disagreements. One control-question
complaint, fair: the byte-for-byte rule and the ledger's in-table misprint
correction read as a contradiction. The charter now names the licence.
