# Term decisions — Leibniz, Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire

The ledger the translation must follow, and the reasons, so a later reader can
disagree with the reason rather than guess at it. Where the ledger and a
translator's judgment collide, escalate with the rationale — the ledger
outranks impulse, not argument.

## The standing policy: cognate first (owner, 2026-09-04)

Wherever reasonable, render a word by its English cognate or by literally the
same word. It is the least invasive option, morphologically faithful, and it
keeps the translation neutral about interpretation — the reader inherits the
author's word, not our paraphrase of it. It also does quiet pedagogical work
in an interlinear: same-shaped words act as visible anchors between the
facing texts, so a reader with no French can still see which sentence answers
which.

**The carve-out is the false friend.** A cognate whose English meaning has
drifted from the author's sense would be the *most* invasive choice while
looking like the least. So: cognate by default, and every departure recorded
here with its reason. The departure list should stay short; if it grows long,
the policy is being evaded.

## Decisions for this text

**caractères → characters.** Cognate, and load-bearing: the final sentence
turns on the Caractéristique, and Leibniz means written signs throughout —
"digits" would smuggle in the modern conclusion.

**Caractéristique → Characteristic.** His technical term, capitalized as he
capitalizes it. Not "characteristic language" or any gloss.

**Table des Nombres → Table of Numbers.** Cognate, capitals as printed.

**la science des Nombres → the science of Numbers.** Same.

**progression → progression.** Cognate. "De dix en dix" → "by tens";
"de deux en deux" → "by twos".

**Essayeurs → Assayers.** This *is* the cognate route by the older door:
essai/assay are the same word split by Anglo-Norman, and the coin-weighing
context settles the modern spelling. Capital as printed.

**Cova stays Cova; Fohy stays Fohy** (glossed once, on first occurrence:
Fohy = Fuxi; Cova = Leibniz's transliteration of gua 卦). His spellings are
part of what the text records — a European learning China through Bouvet's
letters — and modernizing them would erase exactly that.

**esprit → mind** in "aider l'esprit humain". DEPARTURE, recorded: "spirit"
is the false friend here — seventeenth-century *esprit* in an epistemic
context is the reasoning faculty, and the sentence is about a calculus that
aids reasoning. The closing phrase of the whole piece; it must not sound
mystical in English when it is cognitive in French.

**monnoyes → coinage.** DEPARTURE, recorded: the cognate "moneys" survives in
English but means sums, not coin-systems; Leibniz's point is about
denominations struck with few distinct pieces.

**vuide → void or empty?** "remplir le vuide au commencement de la colonne"
— "to fill the empty space at the head of the column". DEPARTURE from bare
cognate "void", which in modern English is too substantival for what is just
blank space in a table. Recorded.

**consentement → agreement.** DEPARTURE, recorded 2026-09-04 — reached
independently by both passes, which is the strongest kind of evidence this
ledger can hold. "Consent" would suggest an act of willing; Leibniz means that
the figures and his Table correspond.

**calcul → calculation; manière de calcul → manner of calculation.** Cognate.
Added at adjudication: pass 1 had "reckoning" as an unrecorded departure and
pass 2 caught it by following policy. Not "calculus", which imports Leibniz's
later technical sense.

**s'écrit par 10 → written as 10.** The one place "by" would mislead: the
numeral is the form the number takes, not the means. Plain in syntax.

**nombres figurés → figurate numbers.** Cognate and the standard term.

**plus d'un millenaire d'années → more than a millennium of years.** His
pleonasm, kept; the policy preserves the author's redundancy rather than
tidying it.

**la Table d'une fois un est un → the Table of once one is one.** The
traditional English name.

**Doubtful readings in the witness are translated as printed and footnoted
with the likelier sense** (seg:9, *pas tout*). Settled at adjudication when
the two passes parted exactly there.

**Misprint handling (from the transcription's ledger):** "Cette expressions"
→ translate the corrected singular, footnote the misprint. The addition
example's swapped 7/6 labels → print corrected, footnote citing the
subtraction below as the internal proof. "prolixtié" → prolixity, footnote.
The witness keeps the error; the translation corrects and says so.

**Orthography of names:** Pekin, Bouvet, Gerbert, Sylvestre II, Maures
d'Espagne → Peking, Bouvet, Gerbert, Sylvester II, Moors of Spain — the
conventional English forms of the same referents; these are not
interpretation.
